## Requirements

Your Wi-Fi network needs to meet these three requirements:

- **2.4 GHz:** The Duo only joins 2.4 GHz networks. Most routers offer 2.4 GHz alongside 5 GHz, often under the same network name.
- **Internet:** The network must be able to reach the internet.
- **No sign-in page:** Guest Wi-Fi that asks you to accept terms or enter a code in a browser won't work.

{% include warn.html text="Wi-Fi with a sign-in page won't work. Use your main home network instead." %}

## Controlling your Duo

Once your Duo is set up, you can control it from the app from anywhere. Your phone doesn't need to be on your Wi-Fi.

## Settings that can block your Duo

Most home routers work with no changes. If your Duo can't connect, check your router for:

- **MAC address filtering:** If it's on, the Duo is blocked until you allow it.
- **WPA3 only:** If your network is set to WPA3 only, switch it to WPA2/WPA3 or WPA2.

## If your connection is weak

If your Duo's network doesn't show up during setup, or the app shows it as Offline, the Wi-Fi signal where the Duo hangs is probably too weak. After any change below, try again from the app.

Wherever you put a router, access point or extender:

- Wi-Fi passes through clear glass. Brick, concrete, stone, metal and tinted or energy-efficient (low-E) glass block it. A plain window facing the patio is usually the best path.
- The fewer walls between the device and the Duo, the better.

{% include dodont.html do="Keep it up high and in the open." dont="Put it in a cabinet, or near metal shelving, appliances or a large metal object." %}

The options below are listed most reliable first.

### Option 1: Move your router or access point

Often a free fix. Move it toward the wall or window facing the Duo. If your router has to stay next to your internet provider's box, go to [Option 2](#option-2-add-an-access-point).

### Option 2: Add an access point

An access point is a second Wi-Fi device, connected to your router by a network cable. Place it indoors by a wall or window facing the patio, or outdoors if you prefer. The Duo has no network port, so the cable runs to the access point, not to the Duo.

- **Already have Wi-Fi equipment, like UniFi or eero:** We recommend adding one more of the same brand, so everything is managed in one app. This isn't required.
- **No brand, or not sure:** See [our recommended product](#our-recommended-product).
- A separate network name, like *Patio-WiFi*, is fine. Pick that network when you set up the Duo.

### Option 3: Add an extender

An extender repeats your Wi-Fi without a cable. It's the simplest to set up, but the least reliable: it depends on a wireless link and a wall outlet.

- Plug it in indoors, about halfway between your router and the patio, where the signal is still strong.
- Make sure it broadcasts on 2.4 GHz.
- Use an outlet that's hard to reach and rarely used: not one for charging phones, and not one on a light switch.

{% include warn.html text="Label the extender: Don't unplug, powers patio heaters. If it loses power, the heaters go offline." %}

If you don't have a preferred brand, we recommend the Netgear EAX15 WiFi 6 Mesh Extender (about $90). It works with most routers and can run as an [access point (Option 2)](#option-2-add-an-access-point) or an [extender (Option 3)](#option-3-add-an-extender). ## No Wi-Fi?

The pull string on your Duo works without Wi-Fi, a phone or the internet. If your Wi-Fi is down, you can still get heat.
